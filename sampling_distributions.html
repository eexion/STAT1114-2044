Exam Decision Tree: \(Z\) vs. \(t\)?

How to choose the correct formula in exam questions:

Step 1: What parameter?
• Mean (\(\mu\)) \(\rightarrow\) Proceed to Step 2.
• Proportion (\(p\)) \(\rightarrow\) Use \(Z = \dfrac{\hat{p}-p}{\sqrt{\dfrac{p(1-p)}{n}}}\).
Step 2: Is \(\sigma\) Known?
• YES (\(\sigma\) known): Use One-Mean \(Z\)-Interval (\(z_{\alpha/2}\)).
• NO (\(\sigma\) unknown, \(s\) given): Use One-Mean \(t\)-Interval (\(t_{\alpha/2},\ df=n-1\)).
Step 3: Normality / Sample Size
• If population is normal, valid for any \(n\).
• If population non-normal, requires \(n \ge 30\) (CLT).
Professor's Common Mistake Alert

Students often confuse sample standard deviation \(s\) with standard error \(SE = \dfrac{s}{\sqrt{n}}\). Remember: standard error shrinks as sample size \(n\) grows!

Key Takeaway for STAT1114 Exam:

When n = 1, the sampling distribution of the sample mean is identical to the population distribution. As the sample size increases, the sampling distribution becomes more symmetric and increasingly resembles a normal distribution — even when the original population is skewed or bimodal — provided the population has a finite mean and finite variance.

This is the essence of the Central Limit Theorem (CLT): the sampling distribution of the sample mean approaches a normal distribution as n increases, regardless of the population's shape (under the finite mean and variance condition). While n ≥ 30 is commonly used as a practical guideline, this threshold is not universally sufficient — highly skewed populations may require a considerably larger sample size before the normal approximation becomes reliable.

Additionally, as n increases, the standard error (\(SE = \dfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}\)) decreases, causing the sampling distribution to become more tightly concentrated around the population mean \(\mu\).

1. Sampling Distribution of Mean (\(\bar{X}\))

Slide 11

Mean: \(\mu_{\bar{X}} = \mu\)

Standard Error: \(\sigma_{\bar{X}} = \dfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}\)

Z-Score: \(Z = \dfrac{\bar{X} - \mu}{\sigma / \sqrt{n}} \sim N(0, 1)\)

2. Sampling Distribution of Proportion (\(\hat{p}\))

Slide 17

Sample Proportion: \(\hat{p} = \dfrac{X}{n}\)

Standard Error: \(\sigma_{\hat{p}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{p(1-p)}{n}}\)

Z-Score: \(Z = \dfrac{\hat{p} - p}{\sqrt{\dfrac{p(1-p)}{n}}} \sim N(0, 1)\)

3. One-Mean Confidence Intervals

Slide 26, 34

\(Z\)-Interval (\(\sigma\) Known):

\(\bar{x} \pm z_{\alpha/2} \left(\dfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\)

\(t\)-Interval (\(\sigma\) Unknown, \(s\) Given):

\(\bar{x} \pm t_{\alpha/2} \left(\dfrac{s}{\sqrt{n}}\right), \quad df = n - 1\)

4. Sample Size Determination (\(n\))

Slide 30

Margin of Error: \(E = z_{\alpha/2} \cdot \dfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}\)

Sample Size Formula: \(n = \left(\dfrac{z_{\alpha/2} \cdot \sigma}{E}\right)^2\)

⚠️ Rule: Always round fractional \(n\) UP to nearest whole number!
