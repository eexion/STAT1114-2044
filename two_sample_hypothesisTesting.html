Mastering Two-Sample Hypothesis Testing

Learn to evaluate differences between two populations across 5 major study designs: Independent $Z$, Pooled $t$, Satterthwaite/Welch $t$, Paired $t$, and Two-Proportion $Z$-tests.

Study Design Foundation: Independent vs. Dependent Samples

Choosing the wrong design leads to invalid degrees of freedom and incorrect variance estimation.

Independent Samples

Two Unrelated Groups

Observations drawn from Population 1 carry zero information about Population 2. No natural pairing or matching exists.

Real-World Examples:
  • Comparing IT salaries in Private vs. Public sectors ($n_1=35, n_2=30$)
  • Testing A/B model conversion rates (Social Media Ads vs. Email Marketing)
  • Comparing SAT scores of Male vs. Female students
Dependent / Paired Samples

Matched Pairs or Repeated Measures

Each observation in Sample 1 is uniquely paired with an observation in Sample 2 (e.g., pre-test/post-test on the same subjects).

Real-World Examples:
  • 10 students tested Before vs. After a 12-week Peer Tutoring programme
  • Calorie intake before and after dietary counselling
  • Paired algorithm benchmarking on identical test instances

Quick reference for all two-sample hypothesis test scenarios in STAT1114 / STAT2044.

Scenario & Test Key Assumptions Test Statistic Formula Degrees of Freedom ($\text{df}$) Confidence Interval Formula
Two-Means $Z$-Test $\sigma_1, \sigma_2$ Known Independent samples, normal distributions or $n_1, n_2 \ge 30$, known $\sigma_1, \sigma_2$. $$z_0 = \frac{\bar{x}_1 - \bar{x}_2}{\sqrt{\frac{\sigma_1^2}{n_1} + \frac{\sigma_2^2}{n_2}}}$$ Standard Normal $Z$
(No $\text{df}$ needed)		 $$(\bar{x}_1 - \bar{x}_2) \pm z_{\alpha/2} \sqrt{\frac{\sigma_1^2}{n_1} + \frac{\sigma_2^2}{n_2}}$$
Pooled $t$-Test $\sigma_1 = \sigma_2$ Unknown Independent samples, normal populations, equal variances assumed ($p_{\text{Folded F}} > \alpha$). $$t_0 = \frac{\bar{x}_1 - \bar{x}_2}{s_p \sqrt{\frac{1}{n_1} + \frac{1}{n_2}}}$$ $$s_p = \sqrt{\frac{(n_1-1)s_1^2 + (n_2-1)s_2^2}{n_1 + n_2 - 2}}$$ $$\text{df} = n_1 + n_2 - 2$$ $$(\bar{x}_1 - \bar{x}_2) \pm t_{\alpha/2, \text{df}} \cdot s_p \sqrt{\frac{1}{n_1} + \frac{1}{n_2}}$$
Satterthwaite (Welch) $t$-Test $\sigma_1 \neq \sigma_2$ Unknown Independent samples, normal populations, unequal variances ($p_{\text{Folded F}} \le \alpha$). $$t_0 = \frac{\bar{x}_1 - \bar{x}_2}{\sqrt{\frac{s_1^2}{n_1} + \frac{s_2^2}{n_2}}}$$ $$\text{df} = \frac{\left(\frac{s_1^2}{n_1} + \frac{s_2^2}{n_2}\right)^2}{\frac{(s_1^2/n_1)^2}{n_1-1} + \frac{(s_2^2/n_2)^2}{n_2-1}}$$ *(Always round DOWN)* $$(\bar{x}_1 - \bar{x}_2) \pm t_{\alpha/2, \text{df}} \sqrt{\frac{s_1^2}{n_1} + \frac{s_2^2}{n_2}}$$
Paired $t$-Test Dependent Samples Paired observations ($d_i = x_{1i} - x_{2i}$), differences are normally distributed. $$t_0 = \frac{\bar{d} - 0}{s_d / \sqrt{n}}$$ $$\text{df} = n_{\text{pairs}} - 1$$ $$\bar{d} \pm t_{\alpha/2, n-1} \cdot \frac{s_d}{\sqrt{n}}$$
Two-Proportion $Z$-Test Categorical Success Rates Independent categorical samples, $n_i \hat{p}_i \ge 5$ and $n_i(1-\hat{p}_i) \ge 5$ for both groups. $$z_0 = \frac{\hat{p}_1 - \hat{p}_2}{\sqrt{\bar{p}\bar{q}\left(\frac{1}{n_1} + \frac{1}{n_2}\right)}}$$ $$\bar{p} = \frac{x_1 + x_2}{n_1 + n_2}, \bar{q} = 1 - \bar{p}$$ Standard Normal $Z$
(No $\text{df}$ needed)		 Not covered in core lecture exam bounds
Folded $F$-Test Equality of Variances Used before choosing Pooled vs. Satterthwaite $t$-test. $H_0: \sigma_1^2 = \sigma_2^2$. $$F_0 = \frac{\max(s_1^2, s_2^2)}{\min(s_1^2, s_2^2)}$$ $$\text{DF}_{\text{num}} = n_A-1$$
$$\text{DF}_{\text{den}} = n_B-1$$		 If $p > \alpha \implies$ Pooled
If $p \le \alpha \implies$ Satterthwaite

10-Minute Timed Lecture Exam Drill

Recreates the lecture script timed individual exam drill. Answer 5 questions under exam conditions (17 marks total).

10:00
Question 1 [3 Marks] Two-Means Z

A sports scientist compares weekly running distance (km) of runners in KL ($n_1 = 40, \bar{x}_1 = 28.4, \sigma_1 = 5.8$) and Penang ($n_2 = 36, \bar{x}_2 = 25.9, \sigma_2 = 6.4$). At $\alpha = 0.05$, test whether the mean weekly running distance differs between the two cities.

Question 2 [4 Marks] Pooled t-Test

Comparing GPA of Morning ($n_1=15, \bar{x}_1=3.41, s_1=0.38$) vs Afternoon ($n_2=12, \bar{x}_2=3.18, s_2=0.42$) students. Assume normal distributions and equal population standard deviations. At $\alpha = 0.05$, test if Morning students have a higher mean GPA.

Question 3 [4 Marks] Satterthwaite t-Test

Comparing monthly transaction amounts (RM) between iOS ($n_1=20, \bar{x}_1=1850, s_1=320$) and Android ($n_2=18, \bar{x}_2=1640, s_2=480$). Assume unequal standard deviations. Compute Welch $\text{df}$ (round down) and test at $\alpha = 0.05$ right-tailed.

Question 4 [3 Marks] Paired t-Test

8 participants calorie intake differences (After - Before): -180, -240, -150, -200, -310, -175, -225, -190. At $\alpha = 0.05$, did counselling reduce calorie intake?

Question 5 [3 Marks] Two-Proportion Z

Impulse purchase survey: 310/380 (18-34 age group) vs 72/120 (35-55 age group). Test if impulse purchase proportion differs at $\alpha = 0.05$.

Question 6 [3 Marks] Folded F-Test

A food analyst compares consistency of protein content across two brands of protein bars. Brand A: $n_1 = 12,\; s_1 = 2.3\text{ g}$. Brand B: $n_2 = 15,\; s_2 = 1.1\text{ g}$. SAS PROC TTEST outputs Folded $F = 4.372,\; p\text{-value} = 0.0082$. At $\alpha = 0.05$, what is the correct conclusion about variance equality and which $t$-test row should be reported?

Question 7 [4 Marks] Pooled t-Test

A manager compares average call handling time between two training programmes. New Training: $n_1 = 18,\; \bar{x}_1 = 4.2\text{ min},\; s_1 = 0.85\text{ min}$. Old Training: $n_2 = 16,\; \bar{x}_2 = 4.9\text{ min},\; s_2 = 0.78\text{ min}$. Folded $F$ $p\text{-value} = 0.68$. At $\alpha = 0.01$, test $H_0: \mu_1 = \mu_2$ (two-tailed).

Question 8 [4 Marks] Satterthwaite t-Test

Delivery time comparison between two suppliers. Supplier A: $n_1 = 10,\; \bar{x}_1 = 5.8\text{ days},\; s_1 = 1.2\text{ days}$. Supplier B: $n_2 = 8,\; \bar{x}_2 = 6.5\text{ days},\; s_2 = 2.4\text{ days}$. Folded $F$ $p\text{-value} = 0.015$. Compute Welch $\text{df}$ (round down). At $\alpha = 0.05$, test $H_0: \mu_1 \geq \mu_2$ vs $H_A: \mu_1 < \mu_2$ (left-tailed — does Supplier A deliver faster?).

Question 9 [4 Marks] Paired t-Test

Ten UX testers rated app usability before and after a redesign. Differences (After − Before): $+12, +8, +15, -3, +10, +6, +9, +14, +2, +7$. At $\alpha = 0.05$, test $H_0: \mu_d = 0$ vs $H_A: \mu_d \neq 0$ (two-tailed). Did the redesign significantly change usability scores?

Question 10 [3 Marks] Two-Proportion Z

Customer satisfaction survey: Store East — 142 out of 200 satisfied ($\hat{p}_1 = 0.710$). Store West — 168 out of 200 satisfied ($\hat{p}_2 = 0.840$). At $\alpha = 0.05$, test $H_0: p_1 \geq p_2$ vs $H_A: p_1 < p_2$ (left-tailed — is Store East's satisfaction rate lower?).
